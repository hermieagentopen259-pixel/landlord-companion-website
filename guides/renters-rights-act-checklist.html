Renters’ Rights Act: property-by-property check

Updated 16 October 2026

Use this once for every property. It is a prompt to inspect your real records and the latest government guidance, not a certificate of compliance.

1. Tenancy

  • □ Identify the current tenancy, tenants and rent period.
  • □ Store the written agreement or the required written statement of terms.
  • □ Record when the Renters’ Rights Act Information Sheet was supplied where required.
  • □ Check that the record does not depend on a fixed end date that no longer operates.

2. Money and advertising

  • □ Record the current rent and last effective increase date.
  • □ Use Form 4A, the section 13 process and at least two months’ notice for a proposed increase.
  • □ Retain the advertised asking rent and agreed rent.
  • □ Confirm rent was not requested or accepted before the agreement was signed.
  • □ Apply applicant criteria consistently without prohibited discrimination.

3. Requests, repairs and evidence

  • □ Log written pet requests, response deadlines, decisions and reasons.
  • □ Keep repair reports, responses, visits, work orders and completion evidence.
  • □ Open every certificate file and verify its property, dates and findings.
  • □ Keep deposit-protection and prescribed-information evidence accessible.

4. If possession is being considered

  • □ Stop and obtain current guidance or legal advice.
  • □ Identify an applicable section 8 ground and preserve its evidence.
  • □ Check the current form, notice period, restrictions and service method.
  • □ Follow the court process if the tenant does not leave.

5. Portfolio review

  • □ Repeat the check for every property and joint landlord.
  • □ Verify a restored backup by sampling records and attachments from each property.
  • □ Review the official implementation roadmap for later duties.
  • □ Date the review and schedule the next one.

Print or save this checklist: use your browser’s Print command. The page has a printer-friendly layout.
